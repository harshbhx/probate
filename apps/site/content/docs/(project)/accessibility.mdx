### How it got there

PageSpeed's first run on phones gave Performance 92. The animated "every night." in the headline changed its width on every frame, so the text around it moved (layout shift 0.15). Agentic browsing (2 of 3) counts the same shift. The animation now sits on top of the real text and never resizes it, so layout shift is 0.

After that fix, and two more that cut the work a docs page does while loading, Lighthouse 13.6 on our own machine gives:

| Page | Accessibility | Best Practices | SEO | Agentic browsing | Performance, phone | Performance, desktop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 91 to 96 | 100 |
| Docs pages | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 93 to 97 | 100 |

The phone Performance range is what our test machine measures for main-thread time, which varies between runs. PageSpeed's own run on the deployed fix is the 100 above.

What changed on the docs pages:

- Each underlined [glossary](/docs/glossary) word used to load its own pop-up code. One shared card per page now shows every definition, so the words themselves need no JavaScript.
- Opening a docs page used to download about 20 other pages for the sidebar links straight away. Pages are now fetched when a pointer, finger or the keyboard reaches a link (6 downloads instead of 20).

## axe on every page

[axe](https://github.com/dequelabs/axe-core) checks a page against the WCAG rules a program can test. We run it on every page of the landing page and docs, at 390 and 1280 pixels wide.

| What | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Pages checked | 48, each at 2 widths |
| WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 A and AA problems | 0 |
| Pages that scroll sideways on a phone | 0 |
| Broken links between pages | 0 of 49 |

With axe's extra best-practice rules on, 28 notes remain, all from one rule (`region`) on parts of the docs framework we don't write: its "skip to page" link and the table of contents sit outside a landmark. They are not WCAG failures.

The live family site was checked the same way on its home, trial, generation, instincts, evidence and judges pages: no problems on any of them.

## Built in

- **Keyboard.** Every link, button and glossary word can be reached with Tab. A focused glossary word opens its definition, and Escape closes it and returns focus to the word.
- **Reduced motion.** With reduced motion turned on in the device settings, nothing animates: the headline is final, numbers show their end values, and the scroll film shows its last frame.
- **No JavaScript.** Every heading and paragraph is in the page's HTML, and a fallback rule shows text that animations would fade in.
- **Plain words.** Hard words are underlined the first time they appear on a page, and hovering or tapping one shows what it means.
- **Dark only.** Both sites have one dark theme, checked for contrast by axe.

## Not tested yet

- Screen readers (VoiceOver, NVDA, TalkBack) and forced-colours mode.
- A keyboard-only walk through the search dialog and the docs menu on phones.
- The live family's attack panel and running-life panel, which need a real Courthouse.
- The product screenshots on the landing page: their small text can't be read on phones, but each has alt text.
- axe can't judge text over the hero glow, or words that are dim on purpose until you scroll to them.

## Report a barrier

If something on these sites is hard or impossible to use, [open an accessibility issue](https://github.com/harshbhx/probate/issues/new?template=accessibility.yml). It asks which page, what you were trying to do and what got in the way; you don't need to know WCAG. Or email harshbhx@gmail.com with "Accessibility" in the subject.

The same statement, with what we aim for and the full test log, is on the repository's **Accessibility** tab on GitHub.

How to repeat every check, and what was fixed along the way, is in [`docs/ACCESSIBILITY.md`](https://github.com/harshbhx/probate/blob/main/docs/ACCESSIBILITY.md).
